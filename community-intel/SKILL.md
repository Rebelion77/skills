# Community Intelligence

Clean-room playbook for reading a crypto project's community as evidence:
is there a real crowd, is it growing or botted, what is it talking about,
and does any of it change a screening or farming decision. Research only —
public sources, no infiltration, no astroturfing, no engagement farming
on the user's accounts without his explicit order.

## 1. Sources (public only)
- X: project account + community posts via web/social search — follower
  count, post cadence, reply quality (real conversation vs giveaway spam).
- Telegram/Discord: only what is publicly visible (member counts, public
  previews, announcement channels). Closed groups are reported as
  "not readable from outside" — never joined under false pretenses.
- GitHub: commit cadence, contributor count, issue responsiveness for
  projects that ship code.
- On-chain corroboration: holder count and growth, unique minters/buyers,
  wash patterns (same wallets cycling). On-chain beats follower counts.

## 2. Signal rubric (score each, show evidence)
- Reality: engagement-to-follower ratio, unique voices vs repeated
  templates, age distribution of accounts. Inflated/botted = say so.
- Demand: organic mentions outside the project's own channels, secondary
  market chatter, waitlist/mint pace versus community size.
- Team presence: do they answer hard questions (delays, tokenomics,
  incidents) or only post hype? Silence during problems is a signal.
- Narrative position: what topic is the community actually riding (AI
  agents, gaming, points meta) and is that narrative rising or fading.
- Red flags: paid-pump patterns, copy-paste shill waves, deleted
  announcements, community discovering problems before the team admits
  them (FUD with receipts beats official reassurance).

## 3. Feeding BAW Hunter screening (primary integration)
Community evidence is a SUPPORTING input to the hunter's POTENSI lane:
- It can strengthen or weaken a POTENSI case (real demand evidence, an
  established community, a team that communicates) — quote the concrete
  observation, never a vibe.
- It NEVER overrides a hard filter: failed on-chain free lane, audit
  BLOCK, or no money path stays failed no matter how loud the community
  is. A big community around a pajangan makes it a loud pajangan.
- Absence of community (no X, no channels, no chatter) is itself a
  finding and must be reported as such, as with Slimelings.
- Output format for verdicts: one or two evidence lines max, sourced
  (platform + what was observed + when), prefixed under the POTENSI
  reasoning so the digest can cite it.

## 4. Feeding farming decisions
For airdrop/points projects the user farms: track sentiment shifts,
snapshot/announcement rumors (labeled unconfirmed), team communication
quality, and community-reported problems (claim bugs, changed rules) —
these change keep/trim/drop calls in `airdrop_intelligence` reviews.

## Rules
- Label every claim: VERIFIED (public metric read directly), OBSERVED
  (seen in public posts, not independently confirmed), or RUMOR.
- Never buy followers, post shills, run sockpuppets, or manipulate the
  communities being measured.
- Small samples are called small: three posts are an anecdote, not a trend.
